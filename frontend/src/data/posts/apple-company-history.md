Apple's history is defined by the products that it has created. However, what this company really excels at, is bringing technologies together in unique ways. Throughout the years, Apple has faced both total collapse and astronomical success, and the company has continually asked itself: how can computers and connected technologies be integrated into regular people's experiences? This includes not only products, but also the services and experiences that surround them.

## A small beginning in California
Apple Computer was formed in 1976, when Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne came together to market Wozniak's new computer design. The first Apple computer was created for people who had an interest in tinkering with circuits and other electronic parts. The next home computer, the Apple II was much more fleshed out and rounded. It was much more approachable and gained popularity amongst corner shops, households, and educational facilities.
### The computer becomes personal
Apple II was immensely popular, and the market for personal computers was soon formed. Moreover, Apple's fame skyrocketed, and the young company soon went public. Apple's popularity exploded, diversifying and expanding far beyond its original core of tech-savvy consumers. It was no longer just a neat circuit board: Apple was creating a new class of product, a personal device, at the dawn of the computer age.
The Macintosh was the next big thing sold by the company. It had a graphical interface with a mouse, and was much more visually-oriented than its predecessor. This was the birth of a new genre of software and user experience design, which made icons and windows an indispensable part of a computer's interface. However, the product's initial offerings could hardly satisfy a growing customer base. It was not cheap, nor was it intuitive, nor did it have the extensive customization options that some of its buyers desired. However, the Macintosh product line and its graphical interface, once again, shaped the company's future direction. The computer was perfect for graphic design, video editing, and other visually-intensive tasks.
## Reinvention and a difficult period
The next few years for Apple were absolutely tumultuous, as Steve Jobs left the company, and Apple's management struggled to find a coherent strategy for the company's development. Though relatively small, the company continued to churn out products, some of which found good reception, while others became technological oddities. The Macintosh continued to dominate the market, Apple's product lines proliferated, with mixed results, and the competition with Windows PCs only grew. The company's fame was at its peak, but it seemed that Apple was losing control over its growth, and the 1990s came as a major setback for the company.
Apple acquired the rights to the NeXT operating system, and Steve Jobs returned to lead the company. He brought a fresh pair of eyes to the problem, and he restructured the company, narrowing its product offering and establishing greater coherence between the products that the company had to offer. These changes were not an instant fix for Apple's problems, but they did position the company for greater stability and growth.
## Design becomes a business strategy
The company's new approach was demonstrated with the release of iMac, which was radically different from the usual bulky computers that one could see in stores. Its bright colors and overall aesthetic made it a great product in terms of both function and form. The iMac represented an important shift in Apple's strategic direction. Its round monitors, combined with the way they interacted with the screen and the keyboard, demonstrated an improved product experience. This was complemented with the increased emphasis on the Internet, which was positioned as an indispensable part of a computer's experience. The iMac was not just about technology and its features, but its design and overall experience were integral to its positioning. The same philosophy was later applied to other products, such as the iPod, which was combined with the iTunes service so that one could easily access the tunes that one wanted.
## The iPhone changes Apple's scale
Apple's next great innovation was the iPhone, which could be used as a mobile phone, an Internet device, and a music player. Simply put, the iPhone was an entirely new category of product, which redefined the way that people interacted with phones. The phone's interface was driven by software, which allowed for greater flexibility compared to a physical button-based interface. With the advent of the App Store, outside developers had the opportunity to create content and services that could be hosted directly on Apple's platform. This was crucial, as it enabled a more diverse set of experiences, and greater value for the company's users. The iPhone became Apple's biggest product, representing the company's strategy as a whole. New iterations of the product were released with greater memory, better cameras, larger screens, and more powerful processors. The iPad represented an expanded offering for the digital experience. The same strategy was followed with Watch and AirPods, which were positioned as everyday accessories that enhanced their users' experiences with technology. These products added new dimensions to the company's offerings, allowing it to become even more integrated with its users' experiences, while also being much more specific and narrow. As a result, the company started to emphasize its values: privacy, accessibilitiy, and the experience of its products and services in the long-term.
## Products, services, and an ecosystem
Apple's current product offering consists of Mac, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, AirPods, Apple TV, and HomePod. There are also operating systems, such as iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and tvOS, and various services, including iCloud, Apple Music, Apple TV, Apple Pay, and the App Store. These products integrate together to create a seamless experience across different platforms and devices. One could send a message from an iPhone to a Mac, view it there, and respond using a laptop. Similarly, photos could be stored across multiple devices. The Apple silicon processors, which started to appear in the company's computers in 2020, are another example of technologies that are integrated into the company's offerings. Overall, Apple has been able to create a closed ecosystem around its products, which enables it to control not only the devices that it produces, but also the applications and technologies that run on them. This strategy has allowed the company to provide its customers with an unparalleled level of quality and integration. However, it also comes at the cost of convenience, as less technically-inclined users might have trouble with some of the software and hardware offerings. Another limitation is the company's control over its products and services, which makes its products more proprietary than those of its competitors.
As mentioned above, the App Store represents a unique opportunity for outside developers, who could sell their products through Apple's platform. However, this strategy comes at a cost as well: Apple's control over these products means that the company is able to dictate the conditions according to which these products can be distributed and used. These restrictions have been one of the primary sources of contention between Apple and its competitors, as well as a regulatory burden.
## Continuing innovation
Apple's legacy is one of innovation in technology and design. While it might seem that creating a new product is what the company is all about, in reality, each new offering represents a new approach to technology and the way that it integrates into people's lives. The first computer, the Apple II, made technology accessible to more people. The next product, the Mac, added a visual aspect to one's experience with computers. Finally, the iPhone introduced applications to the world of cell phones.
It is possible to speculate about the direction in which Apple is likely to move in the future. While there are no guarantees, some products and technologies that the company might pursue have already been defined by its strategies and offerings. Artificial intelligence, privacy, health, and the environment are some areas in which Apple is likely to continue to innovate and invest.
